Why corallivory tracks depth in butterflyfishes

The negative relationship between corallivory and depth in the Andriotis et al. butterflyfish manuscript is the expected outcome of light limiting where reef-building corals can live. It is a resource constraint, not an evolutionary association that needs explaining.

Michael Alfaro, 2 November 2026. Page numbers refer to the open-access PDFs linked under each paper.

What the manuscript reports

Test. A phylogenetic logistic regression of corallivory (facultative plus obligate, versus non-coral feeding) on mean depth from FishBase, with no other predictor.

Result. β = −0.0137 ± 0.0059, z = −2.335, P = 0.020: the probability of corallivory declines with depth.

Categorical version. Depth binned as shallow (≤20 m), mid (21–50 m) and extreme (>50 m). Obligate corallivores fall mostly in the shallow bin (16 of 23). Non-coral feeders are described as concentrated in deep environments (26 of 53, under half).

Diet. Obligate corallivores take nearly 100% of bites from scleractinian corals, and Acropora is taken at a higher rate than Montipora (manuscript Fig. 4b).

The argument in five steps

  1. 1

    Reef-building corals are photosymbiotic. Their depth distribution is set by light, and the shallow-to-mesophotic community transition sits near 40 m in clear water and shallower elsewhere (Tamir et al. 2019; Laverick et al. 2017).

  2. 2

    The branching corals that obligate butterflyfishes eat are shallow specialists. Acropora and Stylophora colonies drop by 67% and 96% between 40 and 50 m in Eilat and are absent below 60 m; staghorn corals on the Great Barrier Reef become scarce below 50 m (Tamir et al. 2019; Muir et al. 2015).

  3. 3

    What coral persists deeper is a different resource. Lower mesophotic communities are sparse, and below 100 m they are almost entirely plating Leptoseris (Englebert et al. 2017). Total cover can stay high locally at 40–60 m, but composition shifts away from the genera obligates prefer (Pérez-Rosales et al. 2022).

  4. 4

    Fish assemblages follow the resource. Corallivores are more common in the shallows and decline with depth across a 2–85 m gradient in Raja Ampat (Andradi-Brown et al. 2021). Within a single reef, obligate hard-coral feeders track live coral cover (Pratchett & Berumen 2008). Mesophotic assemblages are dominated by planktivores and invertivores (Fukunaga et al. 2016).

  5. 5

    The manuscript's extreme bin (>50 m) therefore begins where the obligates' prey runs out, and its deep lineages (Prognathodes, Roa) live on rubble and walls at 45–216 m (Pyle & Kosaki 2016; Copus et al. 2019; Baldwin et al. 2018). A negative depth coefficient is close to guaranteed by resource distribution, with no coral-availability covariate in the model to say otherwise.

What this means for the manuscript

Nothing in the depth analyses controls for coral availability, and in practice nothing could: for these fishes, coral availability is the main thing depth measures. So the regression cannot distinguish an evolutionary association from a resource constraint, and the resource constraint alone predicts the sign.

  1. Keep the depth thread, reframed. State that corallivory is confined to the photic zone where branching corals occur, so the depth–diet coupling is expected. The content worth keeping is the ordering of transitions: no direct shallow-to-extreme moves, and obligate corallivory entered only through facultative corallivory.
  2. Move the phyloglm to the SI or drop it. The PLS and pGLS already carry the depth signal with far better support, and the regression is never discussed.
  3. If a depth–diet test is wanted, make it one resource availability cannot explain. Either fit Pagel's correlated-evolution model on diet and depth as binaries and read the transition order (lose corallivory then go deep, or go deep then lose it), or refit the regression on species with mean depth of 50 m or less. A decline within the zone where coral exists would be a result.
  4. Soften the wording. Non-coral feeders in the deep bin are 26 of 53, under half, so “concentrated” overstates it.
